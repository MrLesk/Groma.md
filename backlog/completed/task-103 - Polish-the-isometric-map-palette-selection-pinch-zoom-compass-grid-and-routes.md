## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
The first blueprint map reads as a dense graphite wireframe on yellowish paper, the selection only marks a roof, a trackpad pinch zooms far slower than the fingers move, the sheet has a hard frame, nothing on the map says which way is west, westward routes visibly leave from the bottom of their source, and most arrows end on containers because relationships are authored at container level. Make the map clean white and grey with the groma green as the only accent, mark the whole selected building and its container, make pinch zoom follow the fingers, add a compass glyph, fade the grid out instead of framing it while keeping the crop-mark glyphs, let routes leave from the side facing their target, author relationships between components wherever a component owns the action, and give the CLI's plain-text view a self-explaining name.
<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:END -->

## Implementation Plan

<!-- SECTION:PLAN:BEGIN -->
1. theme.ts: white and neutral-grey light palette plus --map-line for geometry strokes at rest and --accent-tint for the context deck; matching neutral dark palette; style.ts strokes move from --ink to --map-line, text stays --ink.
2. style.ts + map.ts: .selected paints every face stroke in the accent with the wash on the roof or slab deck (islands get the stroke only); map.paint keeps a building -> slab-or-island, slab -> island map and select() toggles .context on that surface (accent stroke, tinted slab deck); hover rules skip selected and context items.
3. camera.ts: wheelFactor(deltaY, pinch) uses rate 0.01 for ctrl+wheel (trackpad pinch) and 0.0015 otherwise; render.ts passes event.ctrlKey; tests cover both rates.
4. map.ts + project.ts + paint-ground.ts: the grid is an endless SVG pattern (one four-cell tile, minor lines per cell, a major line every fourth) filling a pane-sized rect behind the camera group; map.move(camera) applies the camera transform to the group and the pattern and keeps the pattern strokes at one screen pixel; the frame polygon and every fade are gone, the four corner ticks stay in the sheet layer; a compass rose (2:1 ellipse, four-point star, filled north, N/E/S/W lying on the plane) stands west of the content on its centre line and its letter anchors join the fitted bounds; the camera no longer animates; tests: ticks frame every island, compass west and inside bounds.
5. route.ts: a start port on a side facing the target costs 0, other sides pay SIDE_PENALTY 24; fixture test with a westward and an eastward route between two components.
6. groma/observed: entry components commands (src/cli.ts), world-loader (src/core.ts), scan (src/scanner.ts), terminal-host (src/view-host.ts), web-server (src/viewers/web/server.ts; not 'server', the scanner's own name for that container), screen (src/viewers/tui/terminal-viewer.ts); containers no longer declare code (the contract gives code to components); every container-level relationship moves onto them, people point at commands, screen and page with a description per viewer; page -> render, typescript-files -> git added; plain-world renamed Plain text view; README start-here stays valid.
7. action-path.ts: a person's target is a launcher when it reaches another of their targets (transitively), so component-level rows keep the command-line flows; fixture test for the chain case.
8. Verify: bun run check, browser (light, dark, selection with context, hover on the selection, ctrl+wheel pinch ratio, compass, endless grid at fit and zoomed with one-pixel lines, route starts on Human architect), groma view <file> lookups, grep for product names; stop the web server before editing src because its scan watcher folds edits into groma/observed.

9. place.ts placeRow: islands in one row along +gx (people at the west end, externals at the east end, centres on one gy line, ISLAND_GAP apart) instead of along the screen's x axis; project.ts: the compass is built in sheet cells west of the westmost island with its needles on the grid's axes (N is -gy, up-right on screen) and the corner ticks are two arms along the grid's axes per corner; tests and docs follow.

10. Fixes after Alex's review of the selected person: no fill ever changes (accent wash and tint removed; buildings carry no pattern, so nothing shows through a roof); line style means origin only (kind dashes removed); one per-plane matrix (ground, left face, right face) derived from project() lays out names, island patterns, tower hatches and compass letters; ports are taken middle-first for departures and arrivals with later routes spreading around the middle, and a back-side departure runs along the roof and drops behind the building past the roof's shadow (ROOF_SHADOW = 14/24 cell per floor, capped at the sibling corridor); selected names go bold; click on empty sheet or Escape deselects and empties the details pane.

11. Containers as pools: SLAB_DEPTH replaces SLAB_RISE; project.ts paints a pool as its ground-level opening (floor) plus the west and north inner walls; routes are 2D lattice points on the ground plane; the router's deck and riser logic is gone.

12. Slabs level with the grid (Alex's negative-height idea): the slab top is the ground, its 3 px thickness hangs below the grid line drawn over the island; routes arrive at any side pointing inward, back sides through the roof-shadow node like departures; the sheet gets a 1.5 px border through the corner ticks and the compass moves into the sheet's west corner; the fit frames the whole sheet.

13. Routes leave and meet sides square on: APPROACH = 2 lanes of straight run out of every port and into every goal (forced runs skip ports whose lanes are blocked or in use), lane reuse costs 24 so a detour beats an overlap.

14. Arrowheads lie on the sheet: a triangle in plane pixels mapped by the ground matrix and turned 0/90/180/270° along the route's last step; FLOOR is 12 px so a roof's shadow is exactly half a cell per floor and every back-side start and tip lands on the lattice at the roof edge.

15. Corrections after review on the live map: arrowheads keep an 8 px screen size at every zoom (the triangle lies on the plane, scaled by the inverse camera zoom on each move); a port away from a side middle costs in proportion to the side length, so between a wide surface and a building the surface gives way and the line meets the building middle without a kink.
